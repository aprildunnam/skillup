Sample 02 / Skills plus tools

used-record-pricing

Read the body and notice how little vinyl knowledge is in it. It's a sequence of tool calls plus five rules that only exist at this shop.

Copilot Studioneeds 2 tools~35 lines

The file

---
name: used-record-pricing
description: Set a shelf price for a used record from a known condition grade and recent comps. Use when the grade is already established and someone asks what to price it at, what it's worth, or how to tag it.
---

# Used record pricing

## When to use this
The condition grade is already known. If it isn't, use
vinyl-condition-grading first, then come back.

## Steps
1. Look up whether we've carried this title before and what it sold for.
   Use the sales history tool.
2. Pull current market comps for this pressing at this grade.
3. Check current stock. If we already have two or more copies, flag it
   and price to move.
4. Apply the shop's pricing rules below.
5. Produce the price card.

## The shop's pricing rules
- Price to the disc grade, not the sleeve grade.
- Never price above the median comp for the same grade.
- Round to the nearest dollar, always ending in 0 or 5.
- Anything over $60 goes behind the counter, not on the floor.
- If a copy has sat longer than 90 days, price the new one 10% under
  what that one is tagged at.

## Output format
**Price:** $XX
**Basis:** [comp median, our history, or floor rule]
**Placement:** floor / behind counter
**Note:** [anything the tagger needs to know, or "none"]

## If a tool fails
Say which lookup failed and give a price range from what you do have.
Do not present a single price you could not verify.

What makes this work

The chain is explicit

The condition grade is already known. If it isn't, use vinyl-condition-grading first, then come back.

One line, three jobs: it scopes the skill, it stops the wrong skill firing, and it tells the agent what to do instead. This is how you keep two related skills from competing. More on skill sets.

Tools are named by what they do

"Use the sales history tool," not "call GetSalesHistoryV2." That phrasing survives a platform migration, a connector rename, and a move from Copilot Studio to a repo. More on the pattern.

The business rules are in the skill, not the connector

Five rules, and every one of them is a fact about this shop rather than a fact about records:

  • Price to the disc grade, not the sleeve grade
  • Never above the median comp
  • Round to 0 or 5
  • Over $60 goes behind the counter
  • 90-day-stale copies set the ceiling for the new one

Those change on the owner's whim. In a connector, that's a code change. In a skill, it's a text edit by the person who made the decision.

It says what to do when a tool fails

Say which lookup failed and give a price range from what you do have. Do not present a single price you could not verify.

The single most skipped section in tool-driving skills, and the one that prevents the worst failure mode: a confident price built on a lookup that silently returned nothing.

The description names the boundary

Use when the grade is already established.

Its sibling, vinyl-condition-grading, says "use before pricing, when the grade isn't known yet." Each description names the other's territory. That's what disjoint scope looks like in practice.
