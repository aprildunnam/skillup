Sample 04 / Saying no properly

consignment-intake

The most valuable thing this skill does is decline to register something. That's not a limitation, it's the feature.

Copilot Studioneeds 3 toolswrites records~60 lines

The file

---
name: consignment-intake
description: Register a new consignment of CDs or vinyl from a local artist, including terms, split, and review date. Use when an artist drops off stock, when someone asks to add a consignment, or when checking whether an intake form is complete.
---

# Consignment intake

## When to use this
An artist is placing stock with the shop on consignment and it needs
registering. Also use to validate an intake form somebody else filled in.

Do not use this for stock the shop has bought outright. That is a
purchase, not a consignment.

## Required fields
An intake is invalid without all of these. Ask for anything missing.
Do not register a partial intake.

- Artist name
- Contact email (this is where payouts go, so it is not optional)
- Title
- Format: LP, 45, CD, or cassette
- Quantity
- Agreed split

## Standard terms
- Standard split is 60/40 in the artist's favor.
- Review date is 12 months from shelf date unless agreed otherwise.
- Unsold stock at review is returned or renewed, artist's choice.

## Anything above standard needs approval
A split above 60/40, a review period over 12 months, or a quantity over
50 units requires the owner's sign-off before registration.

When that happens, offer two options and recommend the second:
1. Register at standard terms now and amend after approval.
2. Hold as pending and flag for approval.

Recommend option 2, because amending a registered split leaves two
records and that is what causes disputes later.

## Steps
1. Collect every required field. Ask for anything missing.
2. Check the agreed terms against the standard terms above.
3. If anything is above standard, stop and offer the two options.
4. Write the record using the consignment register tool.
5. Confirm back with the registered values.

## Output format
**Consignment intake - [REGISTERED or PENDING APPROVAL]**
Artist: [name]
Contact: [email]
Title: [title]
Format: [format], [quantity] copies
Split: [split] (artist favor)
Shelf date: [date, or "on approval"]
Review date: [date, or "12 months from shelf date"]
[If pending: Flagged to / Reason]

## References
For non-standard split structures, see `reference/split-terms.md`.

What makes this work

Required fields are stated as a hard gate

An intake is invalid without all of these. Ask for anything missing. Do not register a partial intake.

Not "try to collect." Not "ideally include." A partial intake is invalid, full stop. Firm language matters here because a model reading "should generally include" will happily proceed without an email address.

It knows what it isn't allowed to decide

A split above 60/40, a review period over 12 months, or a quantity over 50 units requires the owner's sign-off before registration.

Three thresholds, each one a case where the shop has been burned. The skill's job isn't to be maximally helpful, it's to know the edge of its own authority. More on that shape.

It recommends, and says why

Recommend option 2, because amending a registered split leaves two records and that is what causes disputes later.

The reason is in the skill. That means when the agent explains the recommendation, it explains it correctly, and the person on the other end learns something instead of just being told.

The boundary line does real work

Do not use this for stock the shop has bought outright. That is a purchase, not a consignment.

Outright purchases and consignments look nearly identical at intake. Without that line this skill fires on both, and registers a purchase as consigned stock. Six months later somebody's owed money they aren't owed.

The rare case is bundled

For non-standard split structures, see reference/split-terms.md.

Non-standard splits come up maybe one intake in twelve. The full terms table sits in a file and costs nothing the other eleven times.

The pattern to steal

Required fields, standard terms, thresholds requiring approval, and an explicit "here are your two options, I recommend this one." That structure works for anything with a contract at the end of it: quotes, discounts, refunds, purchase orders, waivers.
