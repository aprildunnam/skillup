Sample 03 / Capturing what one person knows

open-mic-runsheet

The ordering rules in this file existed nowhere except in two people's heads. That's the entire reason it's worth building.

SharePoint1 listbundled template~50 lines

The file

---
name: open-mic-runsheet
description: Build the Thursday open mic run of show from the sign-up list, including set order and sound check times. Use when someone asks about the open mic lineup, running order, who is playing when, the wait list, or the Thursday night schedule.
---

# Open mic run sheet

## When to use this
Sign-ups have closed for a Thursday night and the run of show needs
building. Also use when someone asks who is on the wait list or why.

## Ordering rules
These are the house rules and they are not negotiable.

1. Never put a first-timer in slot 1. Slots 3 and 4 are the first-timer
   slots. They get a warm room and an easy exit.
2. Full bands go last. Teardown stalls the night otherwise.
3. Anyone bumped from the previous week is slotted before new sign-ups,
   regardless of when they signed up.
4. Nobody plays two weeks running if it means bumping someone who did not
   play last week.
5. Solo sets are 12 minutes. Full bands get 20.

## Steps
1. Read the sign-up list for the date.
2. Separate first-timers, returning regulars, and full bands.
3. Apply the ordering rules to fill 11 slots.
4. Everyone who does not fit goes on the wait list, in sign-up order,
   with anyone bumped last week marked for automatic slotting next week.
5. Allocate sound check: 5 minutes per solo act, 20 minutes for a band,
   working backward from doors at 7:30.

## Output format
Use `templates/runsheet.md` exactly. Do not reformat the table.

After the table, always include:
- **Wait list:** names, in order
- **Wait list rule:** who gets automatic slotting next week and why
- **Sound check:** the window, and any act needing more than 5 minutes

## Rules
- Mark first-timers in the Notes column as FIRST TIME, in caps.
- If the sign-up list has more than 15 names, say so before building.
  That is a busy night and the owner wants to know.
- Never drop somebody from the wait list silently. If they were bumped,
  the run sheet has to say so.

What makes this work

The rules are the deliverable

Five ordering rules, and not one of them is obvious:

  1. First-timers never go first, they go third or fourth
  2. Full bands go last so teardown doesn't stall the night
  3. Bumped-last-week beats signed-up-earlier
  4. Nobody plays two weeks running at somebody else's expense
  5. Solo 12 minutes, band 20

Every one of those is a decision somebody made years ago for a good reason. Written down, they survive that person leaving. That's the whole value proposition of this skill and it has nothing to do with AI.

The format lives in a template, not the body

Use templates/runsheet.md exactly. Do not reformat the table.

The sound engineer owns that file. When they want a column added they edit the template, not the skill. Separating the shape of a document from the procedure that produces it means two different people can own two different things. More on bundles.

It refuses to fail silently

Never drop somebody from the wait list silently. If they were bumped, the run sheet has to say so.

Nobody asked for this. It came out of writing rule 3 down properly: if bumped-last-week gets priority, something has to record who got bumped. The skill ended up more considerate than the process it replaced, which happens more often than you'd think.

It escalates instead of guessing

If the sign-up list has more than 15 names, say so before building. That is a busy night and the owner wants to know.

A threshold plus a notification. Two lines. This is the cheapest kind of business logic to encode and one of the most useful.

The description covers five phrasings

"lineup," "running order," "who is playing when," "wait list," "Thursday night schedule." Nobody says "run of show" except the person who wrote the skill, which is exactly the trap Level 200 warns about.
