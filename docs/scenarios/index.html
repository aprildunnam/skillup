The showcase

Levels 100 through 400 teach you the parts. This section shows five finished machines, so you can see where the parts go.

The shop

April's Acoustic Cafe is a used record store with a six seat espresso bar in the middle, three lesson rooms in the back, and an open mic every Thursday. Eleven staff. One owner who is very tired.

None of this is real. All of it is the kind of work that skills are actually for: repeatable, house-specific, written down nowhere, and currently living in one person's head.

What's on the floor

Roughly 6,000 used LPs and 45s, a small new-release wall, consignment stock from about thirty local musicians, a coffee counter, a lesson studio with four teachers, and a storefront that sells maybe a dozen records a week online.

The five scenarios

Each one shows the same four things: the job, what breaks without skills, the skills, tools, and knowledge that make it work, and what actually comes out the other end.

Crate to shelf

A crate of used vinyl comes in Tuesday. By Thursday it needs to be graded, priced, tagged, listed online, and on the floor. Four people currently do this four different ways.

Open mic night

Fifteen sign-ups, eleven slots, one sound check window, and a promo post that has to go out Wednesday. Every week. Currently a text document somebody rebuilds from scratch.

Consignment intake

Local musicians drop off stock on consignment. Terms, splits, payout schedules, and a paper trail that has to hold up when somebody asks about a check six months later.

The lesson studio

Four teachers, sixty students, weekly progress notes to parents. The notes are either three words or three paragraphs and there is no in between.

Counter service

The cafe side. Daily specials board, par-level checks, and a supplier reorder email that has to go out before 3pm or the delivery slips a week.

How to read these

Each scenario page has the same four sections, on purpose. By the third one you'll be reading the shape rather than the words, which is when the pattern starts transferring to your own work.

The job

What somebody at the shop actually has to get done, and how often.

What breaks

The specific failure without a skill. Usually inconsistency, not incapability.

The build

Which skills, which tools, which knowledge, and why each thing is the thing it is rather than one of the others.

What comes out

The actual output, so you can judge whether it was worth building.
